# Presentation concepts

## 1. Signal Atlas (selected)

Dark charcoal canvas, cyan traces, a fine grid, and oversized editorial typography. Each slide uses one research visual: ticker trace, observation coverage, detector window, flow, interface map, or outcome comparison. The visual grammar follows a surveillance analyst's movement from raw activity to a review decision. This best fits the technical audience and exposes caveats without shrinking them into footnotes.

Palette: midnight `#071019`, slate `#14232f`, cool white `#eaf6f5`, muted blue `#8ca8b4`, cyan `#46d8d1`, warm amber `#f2b46e`.

## 2. Research Ledger

An academic lab-book treatment with ivory pages, black type, teal annotations, and plots framed as exhibit plates. Strong for professors and print, but less legible on a bright projector and less suited to the command-center subject.

## 3. Exchange Panorama

Immersive ticker-band motion and panoramic city/exchange abstractions around sparse statements. Strong opening spectacle, but it risks overstating the detector's performance and placing too little evidence on the evaluation slide.

## Selected structure

1. Cover: research title and exchange-ticker field.
2. Problem: market activity exceeds scarce analyst attention.
3. Data: actual scale and yearly observation coverage.
4. Engine: two z-score channels, 60 prior observations, GIF example.
5. Pipeline: evidence flow to human review; Flask and optional terminal.
6. Product: real local application captures, repository-backed features, local demo status.
7. Results: the 1,414 to 16 funnel, 1/3 recall, zeros at k, baseline comparison.
8. Impact: usable screening workflow and research improvements.
